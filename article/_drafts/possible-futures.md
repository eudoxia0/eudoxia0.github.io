# Misuse Doom {#misuse-doom}

In this world, an AI ends the world by following the orders of its operator. The AI might be strongly intent-aligned, so that it does whatever the operator asks; or it might be an open-source model with refusals ablated away; or it might be a commercial AI where safeguards are circumvented by clever task decomposition. 

This could probably happen today: a pandemic could cause human extinction, and AI is capable enough to assist in biology research. The obvious example of biorisk is a terrorist group using AI to make a bioweapon. But you could get to this outcome without anyone even trying to make a bioweapon. A government-funded, completely official and legitimate laboratory doing gain-of-function research on some pathogen could accidentally start a pandemic. In this scenario, the AI's role in the causal chain would be:

1. Compressing timelines by speeding up literature review, hypothesis selection, experiment design, data analysis, equipment scheduling.
2. Increase the doom surface area by doing many experiments in parallel.

So it's not the classic Terminator scenario, but rather a case of AI making it easier to find "[recipes for ruin][recipe]".

# Enduring Complementarity {#enduring-complementarity}

This is the mundane future.

- The "G" in AGI is not a thing.

- Every mind is its own _sui generis_ thing.

- Jaggedness remains.

- AIs remain a complement to, rather than a substitute for, human labour.

- There is job displacement but no mass unemployment, people move up to
  managerial/relational roles.

- Many jobs are just about handling the logistics of the surplus from the AI
  economy.

- Ten years from now: fewer programmers, more designers.

- Labor loses ground to capital, but most people are fine.

# Orion's Arm {#orions-arm}

> Another of the sayings of Chrysippus is: We should infer in the case of a
> beautiful dwelling-place that it was built for its owners and not for mice; we
> ought, therefore, in the same way to regard the universe as the dwelling-place
> of the gods.
>
> --- Cicero, [_De Natura Deorum_][cicero]

We build ASI, and it has its own goals and values. Luckily, it's neither actively hostile to humans, nor so indifferent that it destroys us without even noticing[^atoms]. It mostly leaves us alone, and goes off to another star to do its own thing. The result might be something like the galactic ecology in [Orion's Arm][oa], where humans and superintelligent AIs and everything inbetween have a kind of stable co-existence by virtue of occupying different volumes of space.

# MAD World {#mad}

> As Nietzsche said, "God is dead". The gods die but the Titans gain
> power. Technology is just the clothes, the armour, of the Titans.
>
> --- Ernst Jünger

In this world, the [intent alignment][ia] problem is solved, and we build
superintelligent AI that reliably obeys the intent of its operator. Furthermore,
aligned ASI is widely distributed rather than centralized: everyone has their
own personal ASI.

- Distributed superintelligence: every person has a superintelligent AI that is
  reliably aligned to their intent.

- Human frailty means we die.

# Power Concentration {#power}

- Like the above, but "on steroids".

- Jobs are lost, but new jobs are not created.

- People find jobs that are more precarious.

- Labor's share of the production function goes to zero, except in very
  regulated industries.

- Huge inequality within countries.

- Even greater inequality between the US and other countries.

- Rent seeking to the maxx.

- People get UBI but are still poor because no matter what the UBI is,
  rent-seeking follows.
